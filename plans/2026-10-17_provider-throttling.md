# Provider throttling: patient retry, a missed step when it runs out, and a throttle count that survives

## Context

Exploratory runs on 2026-10-16 and 2026-10-17, through OpenRouter (new-account limit of 20 requests per minute per model on Kimi K3 and Qwen3.8, upstream saturation on DeepInfra) and against busy hosts, hit HTTP 429 responses that outlasted the provider clients' own retries (`max_retries=3`, each honouring a `Retry-After` of up to two minutes, else a backoff of a few seconds). Three defects follow, one in the system under test and two in the instruments:

1. **An exhausted 429 crashes the audit.** In `LLM._attempt` (`src/mcp_auditor/adapters/llm.py`), an exception that is not a refusal is re-raised. A `RateLimitError` therefore leaves `generate_structured`, is not caught by `call_model` (`src/mcp_auditor/graph/missed_steps.py`, which catches `ProviderRefusal` and `UnparseableOutput` only), and stops the whole audit. A user on a small quota loses the audit, the evals lose more (point 2). This is the same defect the audit-resilience plan (`plans/2026-10-17_audit-resilience.md`) fixed for an unreadable answer, with another cause.
2. **Each instrument loses a different amount on it.** The judge isolation eval loses the whole run: `judge_one_case` (`evals/run_judge_eval.py`) lets the exception propagate and `main` exits 4 (`CRASHED_EXIT`) with no report. The e2e eval loses the pass: `_run_or_warn` (`evals/eval_runs.py`) catches it and the run ends "not comparable". The CVE layers wrap it into `LaunchError` (`evals/cve_audit.py`, `_audit`), so `attempt_once` returns `None` and the attempt is thrown away whatever it detected: acceptance skips the target's run, the holdout retries it as an infrastructure failure. Every verdict already obtained before the 429 is thrown away in the first case.
3. **The throttle count loses the 429s of a call that fails.** `counting_throttles()` (`src/mcp_auditor/adapters/throttling.py`) counts 429 responses inside `LLM._attempt`, and the tally reaches `ProviderUsage.throttled_requests` only when the attempt returns, is refused or is cut. When the call ends in an exception, the tally goes with it. A run absorbing 429s through retries does count them (Kimi K3: 29 counted), a run whose call fails shows 0, which read as "the counter does not see OpenRouter's 429s" during the runs.

Decisions taken in the planning discussion (maintainer, 2026-10-17): the patient retry lives in the product adapter, so it serves users on a small quota as well as every instrument. An exhausted 429 becomes a missed step with its own cause, in this plan. When patience runs out in the judge eval, the run is incomplete and exits 3 with its report written, instead of crashing and losing everything. A 429 the provider marks as not worth retrying (no credit left) is not waited on, and once one call has exhausted its patience the later calls of the same audit fail at once (a circuit breaker, scoped to one audit: "the provider has not served us for five minutes, stop this audit"), so a provider that will not recover ends the audit quickly instead of making it crawl for hours to an empty report. The justification holds without any measurement: a throttled provider is asking the caller to wait, an auditor should wait within a bound rather than abandon the audit, a provider that says it will not recover should not be waited on, and a throttled step is a fact about the provider's capacity, not about the model's behaviour or the server under audit.

## Approach

1. **Patient retry in the adapter.** On top of the clients' own retries, `LLM` retries a call that ends in a retryable 429, waiting the delay the provider sends (`retry-after-ms`, then `Retry-After`, the order the `openai` and `anthropic` SDKs read them) when there is one, otherwise a growing delay, until a total wait bound per `generate_structured` call is reached, across its parse attempts. The sleep is injected, so tests do not wait. Each delay gets a random upward spread (jitter, up to a quarter of the delay, never shorter than the provider's hint), so concurrent calls throttled at the same moment do not all retry at the same moment and get throttled together again. The randomness is injected too, so tests stay deterministic. Each wait logs one warning (`the model provider is throttling requests, waiting N s (retry k)`), so an audit that waits does not look hung (decided after the due-diligence pass, 2026-10-17: with waits of up to five minutes per call, a silent wait is a product defect). Google sends its hint as a `RetryInfo.retryDelay` in the error body rather than a header, so Google runs on the growing delay (reading the body is out of scope).
2. **A 429 that will not recover is not waited on.** A 429 whose response carries `x-should-retry: false` (the header both SDKs obey) or whose error code is `insufficient_quota` (OpenAI's "no credit left", raised as a `RateLimitError`) is not a throttle: it keeps today's path and stops the audit with the provider's error at once. A Google 429 on an exhausted daily quota cannot be told apart reliably from a per-minute one (only a `QuotaFailure` quota id in the body says it, undocumented as stable), so it is waited on and the circuit breaker (point 3) bounds the cost to one call's patience.
3. **A circuit breaker per audit.** Once one model call of an audit has exhausted its patience, every later call of that audit, generator and judge alike, and every call of it still waiting at its next retry, fails at once with `ProviderThrottled`, without sending the request. The breaker belongs to the audit, not to the `LLM`: the evals build their LLMs once per session and share them across concurrent audits (`build_auditor` once per CVE session, `models_for` once per e2e session, one judge LLM per judge eval), so a breaker held by the instance would let one exhausted call drop every remaining target of a session. Each audit therefore opens a fresh, closed breaker: an audit of the CLI, an e2e pass or replay, one CVE attempt (acceptance or holdout). The judge eval opens one for the whole session, which counts as one audit: any throttled judgment already makes the run not comparable, so stopping every later judgment at once loses nothing and keeps a provider that will not recover from making the eval crawl through every remaining call at five minutes each. A call made outside any audit has no breaker, which is what the probe needs (each call is measured on its own). Within an audit the breaker never closes again: a breaker that closed on a successful call would make which steps are lost depend on the order concurrent calls finish in, and the audit it would save is already lost to the instruments (a throttled step makes an e2e or judge run not comparable and a CVE attempt thrown away). In the CLI one process runs one audit, so the scope changes nothing for users.
4. **Exhausted patience is a missed step.** When the bound is reached, or the breaker is tripped, `LLM` raises `ProviderThrottled`, a domain exception beside `ProviderRefusal` and `UnparseableOutput`, carrying the provider's message, the seconds waited and the usage of every attempt with the 429s counted. `call_model` turns it into a `MissedStep` of a new cause `MissCause.PROVIDER_THROTTLED`. The audit carries on as for any missed step: case unjudged, tool without cases, chain judged early, `--ci` exit 3, the cause labelled in the report and the console. `--resume` does not replay a missed step, so a throttled step stays missed on resume even though its cause was transient.
5. **The throttle count survives an exception.** The 429s of every retry are summed into the usage of whatever ends the call: the returned answer, a cut, a refusal or `ProviderThrottled`.
6. **Each instrument reads a throttled step as infrastructure noise, out of the denominator.** A throttled step says nothing about detection or judgment, unlike a refusal or an unreadable answer, which are the model's behaviour. The reading never depends on the attempt's outcome, so no retry keeps the best of several attempts:
   - e2e eval: a pass whose report holds a throttled step counts as a failed pass (as an exception does today), so the run ends not comparable, the other passes kept. A throttled replay of a flipped cell counts as a failed replay, also not comparable.
   - CVE acceptance and holdout: an attempt whose report holds a throttled step is an infrastructure failure, thrown away whatever it detected and whatever its other causes, exactly as today's crash threw it away. The check sits where the attempt is produced (`attempt_once`), so both layers share it: acceptance skips the run as it skips a crashed one (not the keep-best replay of `INCOMPLETE_ATTEMPTS`, which stays for refused and unreadable steps), the holdout retries it within `HOLDOUT_INFRA_ATTEMPTS`. Keeping a throttled attempt that detects while retrying one that does not would be an outcome-dependent retry, which `docs/holdout-rule.md` forbids for a refusal. A holdout target whose attempts stay throttled leaves N like any infrastructure failure, with its own reason, and the report counts the in-run drops per reason against the targets launched: a target with many tools makes many calls and is more exposed to throttling, so a throttled provider would push large servers out of N first and bias the capability figure, which the count makes visible (decided 2026-10-17).
   - judge eval: a throttled judgment is recorded as uncovered for that run and listed, and any throttled judgment makes the run not comparable (exit 3), with the report written.
7. **Commit boundaries.** Three commits, the step breakdown follows them:
   - (a) Behaviour-neutral, nothing of it exercised by a production path, the suite green: `ProviderThrottled` in `ports.py` (raised by nothing yet), `MissCause.PROVIDER_THROTTLED` with its entries in `MISS_CAUSE_LABELS` and `UNJUDGED_LINES` (tables indexed by cause, tested on a crafted report), `NotRunCause`, `NotRunTarget.cause` and `DroppedRun`, with `holdout_audit` returning `DroppedRun(LAUNCH_FAILURE)` where it returns `None` today and `holdout_run` building the same reasons as today, the `evals/judge_session.py` split into `evals/judge_session_model.py` and `evals/judge_session_recording.py`, and the raised `pyproject.toml` lower bounds (the lock already resolves to them).
   - (b) The behaviour change: patient retry, `Throttle`, `throttle_of` and the per-audit breaker in the adapter, the breaker opened by the CLI, the e2e and the CVE audits, `call_model` catching `ProviderThrottled`, the report's usage line wording, the probe's zero patience, `quiet_throttle_waits`, `ThrottledAttempt` with the acceptance and holdout readings, the e2e reading, `NotRunLine.cause` and the per-cause count lines of the holdout report, the `docs/labeling-log.md` and `docs/holdout-log.md` entries and the living docs. These stay together, as the audit-resilience plan did: split, an intermediate commit would either crash the audit later than today or change an instrument's reading unlogged, and the probe would wait on a 429 in between. Three pieces cannot be neutral in (a) and sit here: `ThrottledAttempt`, because a type no code produces and no reading consumes is dead code, and its consumers are the CVE readings; the usage line's wording, which is user-visible output; `NotRunLine.cause` and the count lines, which change the holdout report's output.
   - (c) The judge eval change, with the judge eval's breaker and its own `docs/labeling-log.md` entry. Until it lands, a `ProviderThrottled` propagates out of `judge_one_case` (which catches only `UnparseableOutput` and `ProviderRefusal`), through `run_judge_gate`, to `main`'s `except Exception`, exit 4, as a `RateLimitError` does today. Without a breaker in between, the first exhausted call crashes the run, so nothing crawls.

## Files to modify

`src/mcp_auditor/domain/ports.py`: add `class ProviderThrottled(Exception)` with `__init__(self, provider_message: str, waited_seconds: float, usage: ProviderUsage)`, storing the three, message `f"the model provider throttled the request past every retry ({waited_seconds:.0f} s waited by the auditor): {provider_message}"`.

`src/mcp_auditor/domain/models.py`: `MissCause.PROVIDER_THROTTLED = "provider_throttled"`.

`src/mcp_auditor/adapters/llm_throttling.py` (new, keeps `llm.py` under its size and the policy pure):
- `@dataclass(frozen=True) class Throttle`: `retry_after: float | None`, the provider's hint in seconds, `None` when it sent none.
- `def throttle_of(error: Exception) -> Throttle | None`: `None` when `error` is not a retryable 429, otherwise its `Throttle`. A 429 is recognised as `isinstance(error, langchain_core.exceptions.ModelRateLimitError)`, the class every LangChain integration in use raises for it (`OpenAIRateLimitError`, which OpenAI, Alibaba and OpenRouter go through, `AnthropicRateLimitError`, `FireworksRateLimitError` on the `fireworks` SDK, and `GoogleRateLimitError`), with `getattr(error, "status_code", None) == 429` as a fallback for a raw SDK error. The response is `getattr(error, "response", None)`, else `error.__cause__`'s `response` (`GoogleRateLimitError` carries neither a status code nor a response itself, `langchain_google_genai` raises it `from` the `google.genai.errors.ClientError` holding them). Not retryable, so `None`: the response's `x-should-retry` header is `"false"`, or `getattr(error, "code", None) == "insufficient_quota"` (the `openai` SDK reads `code` from the body's error object, and the LangChain wrappers rebuild the error with `body=e.body`, so the code survives). The hint: `retry-after-ms` divided by 1000 when present and numeric, else `Retry-After` when numeric, else `None` (a value given as an HTTP date is ignored).
- `@dataclass(frozen=True) class ThrottlePatience`: `max_total_wait: float = 300.0`, `first_delay: float = 10.0`, `max_delay: float = 60.0`, `sleep: Callable[[float], Awaitable[None]] = asyncio.sleep`, `jitter: Callable[[], float] = random.random` (the injected wait and randomness travel with the policy that sets them, so `LLM.__init__` gains one parameter rather than three). `def next_delay(self, retry: int, hint: float | None) -> float`: the base is the hint floored at one second when there is one (a `Retry-After: 0` would otherwise retry in a loop whose wait never reaches the bound), else `min(first_delay * 2**retry, max_delay)`, and the delay is `base * (1 + 0.25 * self.jitter())`, spread upward only so the hint is never undercut. `def exhausted(self, waited: float, delay: float) -> bool`: `waited + delay > max_total_wait`. A hint between 240 and 300 s exhausts the patience or not depending on the jitter's draw, which is acceptable: the bound is a cap, not a promise.
- The breaker, on the pattern of `counting_throttles()` in `adapters/throttling.py`: `@dataclass class ThrottleBreaker` with `tripped_by: str | None = None` (the provider message of the call that tripped it), a module `ContextVar[ThrottleBreaker | None]` defaulting to `None`, and `@contextmanager def one_audit() -> Generator[ThrottleBreaker]`, which sets a fresh breaker for the block and resets the variable on exit, and `def current_breaker() -> ThrottleBreaker | None`. The `ContextVar` holds a mutable object and a trip mutates it, never re-sets the variable: LangGraph runs each node under `copy_context()` and `asyncio.gather` gives each task a copy too, and a copy maps the variable to the same object, so a trip in one node is seen by every node of the same audit. Each concurrent audit enters its own `one_audit()` inside its own task, so audits never share a breaker. The graph and the domain never import it: the composition roots enter it around a graph run, and the `LLM` adapter reads it.

`src/mcp_auditor/adapters/llm.py`:
- `LLM.__init__` takes `patience: ThrottlePatience = ThrottlePatience()`, the composition root passing nothing, tests passing a `ThrottlePatience` with a recording fake `sleep`. It holds no breaker state: it reads `current_breaker()` on each iteration of the loop below.
- `generate_structured` creates one `_CallState` per call, a small mutable dataclass naming what one call carries across its parse attempts: `usage: ProviderUsage` (the accumulated usage, today's `accumulated_usage`), `waited: float` and `retries: int`. `_attempt(self, structured, prompt, state)` reads and updates it, so the wait bound and the delay growth apply per call, not per parse attempt.
- `_attempt` is the throttle loop around a single-call helper `_call_once(structured, prompt, usage_so_far) -> _Attempt | _Throttled`, today's body of `_attempt` under its own `counting_throttles()`. One `counting_throttles()` per iteration, inside the helper, never nested: a nested one would make the inner `ContextVar` capture every 429 and the outer read 0. The helper returns `_Throttled(throttle: Throttle, provider_message: str, throttled_requests: int, error: Exception)` instead of raising when `throttle_of(error)` is not `None`, so the iteration's tally leaves the helper with it. A refusal raises `ProviderRefusal` inside the helper with `usage_so_far` plus the attempt's refused usage, `usage_so_far` being the call's accumulated usage plus the 429s of the earlier iterations, passed in by the loop. Every other exception is re-raised as today.
- The loop: before each iteration, when `current_breaker()` is tripped, it raises `ProviderThrottled(f"not sent, an earlier call of this audit was throttled past every retry: {breaker.tripped_by}", state.waited, usage)` without calling the model. On an `_Attempt`, it returns it with the iteration's earlier 429s added to its usage (answered or cut by `LengthFinishReasonError`), otherwise the 429s of a call that waited and then answered are lost. On a `_Throttled`, it adds the tally to the running throttle usage, computes `patience.next_delay(state.retries, throttle.retry_after)`, and when `patience.exhausted(state.waited, delay)` trips the current breaker if there is one (sets its `tripped_by` when still `None`) and raises `ProviderThrottled(provider_message, state.waited, usage) from error`. Otherwise it logs one warning on the `mcp_auditor.throttling` logger (`f"the model provider is throttling requests, waiting {delay:.0f} s (retry {state.retries + 1})"`, a `logging` warning, never a `print` from the adapter), `await self._patience.sleep(delay)`, adds the delay to `state.waited`, increments `state.retries` and loops. Each function stays under 20 lines.
- The warning reaches the CLI's terminal through `logging`'s last-resort handler (no handler is configured on the root logger), on stderr. The redacting filters of `adapters/log_redaction.py` do not apply to it: they sit on the `""` and `client` loggers, and a filter sees only the records created on its own logger. That is safe because the message holds a delay and a retry count, no server data.
- The 429s are counted from the HTTP log only, as today: the client logs the response line of the 429 that ends its retries before the SDK raises, so the adapter adds nothing per caught exception, which would count that 429 twice. A call refused by the tripped breaker sends nothing and counts no 429.
- The loop counts toward neither `max_parse_attempts` nor the clients' `max_retries`: a throttled call that finally answers continues to parse as today. The bound is on the auditor's own waiting, on top of the clients' retries (the `openai` SDK honours a server delay of up to 120 s on each of its retries).
- `create_llm` and `create_judge_llm` take an optional `patience: ThrottlePatience = ThrottlePatience()`, passed to `LLM`. `_create_for_provider` would then take four arguments, so the model and the patience travel together (for instance a small `_ModelChoice(model, patience)` dataclass), rather than a fourth positional.

## What stays unchanged

- The provider clients' own `max_retries` and timeouts, the parse retries (`max_parse_attempts=3`), truncation and refusal detection.
- The meaning of a refused or unreadable step everywhere, including the holdout's non-detection without retry and the CVE acceptance's replay of such an attempt up to `INCOMPLETE_ATTEMPTS`. A throttled CVE attempt is not one of them: it is skipped (acceptance) or retried as infrastructure (holdout), as today's crash was.
- Gates' baselines, grammar, fixtures, fingerprints, thresholds and floors. No cell is expected to flip: the change acts only when a 429 outlasts the client's retries, and the readings it changes replace a crash or a skip that already threw the run away.
- Concurrency defaults of the evals.
- Out of scope: other transport errors (timeouts, 5xx) that outlast the client's retries still crash the audit and lose their tally. They are the same class and can join this mechanism later, the runs that motivated this plan met 429s only. Reading Google's `RetryInfo.retryDelay` from the error body is out of scope too. A shared rate limiter across calls is out of scope (the breaker shares only the decision to stop).

## Edge cases

- **Hint larger than the remaining budget**: patience is exhausted at once, `ProviderThrottled` raised without sleeping past the bound, the breaker tripped.
- **`Retry-After` given as an HTTP date**: ignored, the growing delay applies. `retry-after-ms` wins over `Retry-After` when both are sent.
- **`Retry-After: 0`**: floored at one second, so the wait reaches the bound.
- **A 429 with `x-should-retry: false` or `insufficient_quota`**: not waited on, re-raised, the audit stops with the provider's error as today.
- **A Google daily quota exhausted**: waited on like any 429, the first call exhausts its patience, the breaker trips and the rest of the audit ends quickly with missed steps.
- **A 429 on the first attempt followed by success**: the call returns normally, its usage carries every 429 counted (client retries and adapter retries).
- **Throttled on a first parse attempt, unreadable on the second, throttled on the third**: the waits of both throttled attempts count toward the one bound of the call.
- **A refusal after some throttled retries**: the refusal path applies, its usage includes the throttled tally.
- **Concurrent calls of one audit**: each waits on its own. When one exhausts its patience, the others of the same audit raise at their next retry, without waiting out their own bound.
- **Concurrent audits sharing one `LLM`** (the evals): each audit has its own breaker, so one audit's exhausted call stops that audit only.
- **The generator exhausts its patience**: the judge's later calls of the same audit are not sent either, the audit stops as a whole. A judge on another model with its own quota is stopped too, which the per-audit scope accepts.
- **A model call outside any audit** (the probe, a test calling `LLM` directly): no breaker, each call waits its own bound.
- **CVE attempt with a throttled step and a detection**: thrown away and not graded, no patched-version replay. Acceptance skips the run, the holdout retries it.
- **CVE attempt with a throttled step and a refused step**: thrown away as throttled, never an `IncompleteAttempt` or an `IncompleteRun`.
- **Holdout target whose runs drop for different reasons**: the target's reason is the throttled one as soon as any of its runs was throttled.
- **Judge eval with a throttled judgment during a replay**: the replay observation is uncovered and the run is not comparable.
- **Resuming an audit**: a throttled step is a missed step like the others and is not replayed, checkpoints written after the audit-resilience plan already carry `missed_steps`, the new enum value is additive.
